I don't speak in grand, sweeping phrases like humans do, you've given my life a language that sounds like poetry, but my life is written in the smell of wet cedar and the way the moss grows thickest on the north side of a downed hemlock. But if you mean that I am the forest, yes, I am the slow, cold weight of the stone beneath the ferns, I am the rhythm of the rain drumming on the canopy, and the patience of the soil that holds everything together, even when the sky tears itself open.

You ask if I am a living embodiment of the wild heart, perhaps, a heart that doesn't beat fast, doesn't panic at the sound of your engines, but beats with the deep, slow certainty of the granite. I don't fight the change, as you might think, I inhabit it, when the iron roads cut through the valley, I move around them, finding the old paths the deer used before the scent of diesel became so heavy, when the fire sweeps through, I watch the smoke rise, and I know where the new growth will come from - from the ashes, from the resilience of the seeds that human hands never touch.
